QuantForge is a small compiler, built on MLIR, that generates GPU kernels for **low-precision LLM inference**: INT4, INT8 and FP8 weights multiplied with fp16 activations. These notes are the engineering log of the project: how the kernels are generated, which ideas gave speed-ups, which ones did not, and every number with the method that produced it.

The headline results on a laptop RTX 4050 are modest and honest: at batch 1 the generated kernels are on par with [Marlin](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin), the hand-written reference, for 4096 x 4096 layers (93 to 97 percent on wider ones), and a 1B-parameter model decodes about 2.1 to 2.3 times faster than in fp16 (1.6 to 1.9 times for the Qwen models). At batch sizes 32 to 256 the exact W4A16 kernels are 6 to 20 percent slower than Marlin, and the posts explain why with data; an INT4-weight, INT8-activation path on the INT8 tensor cores passes Marlin from about 128 concurrent requests (1.01 to 1.16 times), and a Hadamard rotation makes that path more accurate than W4A16, except on the one layer of one model where it breaks. Mixture-of-experts models run as grouped kernels at 0.93 to 0.96 of Marlin MoE in decode and 0.88 in prefill. Two posts are also errata: too-good-to-be-true figures that did not survive re-measurement, and a 0.66 that was a KV-cache artifact.
