Against other kernels (gemlite, bitsandbytes)#

QuantForge is on par with gemlite at M = 1 (49.9 / 49.3 µs), ahead of it for M = 4 … 2048 (gemlite needs 100 µs at M = 4 and 61 µs at M = 16 where QuantForge needs 50), and 5 % behind it on the wide decode shape 1 × 4096 × 11008 (135.7 against 129.4 µs, after the wave-aware K tile described in PIPELINE.md; it was 149.5 µs before). Marlin is the fastest kernel from M = 32 up. The Triton kernel's strength is its autotuned decode path; its prefill kernel is ~15 % slower than QuantForge's.

End to end against vLLM#

vLLM 0.25.1 (offline engine, CUDA graphs, one request, 256 new tokens, best of 3; benchmarks/vllm_e2e.py) on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct: fp16 165 tokens/s, the official GPTQ-Int4 checkpoint with Marlin kernels 310 tokens/s (1.88× faster). QuantForge inside a plain Hugging Face model with a static cache and a CUDA graph: fp16 129, INT4 (+ INT8 head) 209 tokens/s (1.62×), 226 with torch.compile (1.75×). In absolute terms vLLM is faster, because its model implementation is fully fused (norms, rotary, residuals, sampling) while the Hugging Face graph launches ~1400 small kernels per step; for the GEMMs themselves the table above shows QuantForge on par with Marlin at batch 1. Running the QuantForge kernels inside vLLM (a plugin) would be the fair end-to-end comparison and is not done.

QuantForge is 3.2–3.7× faster than an fp16 cuBLAS GEMM at decode (it moves a quarter of the bytes) and 0.67–1.01× of Marlin. The "≥ 90 % of Marlin" goal is reached for decode (M ≤ 16): 4096 × 4096 (0.99–1.01×), 1 × 11008 × 4096 (0.97×), 1 × 4096 × 11008 (0.93×) and 16 × 4096 × 11008 (0.94×); it is not reached for M = 32 … 256 (0.67–0.87×; M = 64 / 128 are the worst: the kernel is limited by the Tensor Core / ldmatrix path at ~50 % tensor-pipe activity, not by DRAM, while Marlin stays memory-bound up to M = 64) and for the prefill GEMMs (0.85–0.86×). Activations are re-touched after the L2 flush (they are L2-resident in a real model, written by the previous layer). At prefill sizes the compute-bound kernel is ~25 % slower than cuBLAS fp16 with group-wise parameters.

Correction (2026-11-20). Earlier versions of this README reported 38.0 µs / 1.30× Marlin for M = 1 and 4 at 4096 × 4096 (and 0.91 / 0.94× for the wide decode shapes). That figure could not be reproduced: re-running the same harness on the very commit that recorded it gives 50.2 µs (0.99×). With the current method the kernel is on the DRAM limit: ncu (dram__bytes_read, caches flushed) shows 9.2 MB read from DRAM for the 8.65 MB of weights and scales, i.e. ~184 GB/s at 50 µs, and a 1 GB flush instead of the 256 MB one changes neither kernel's time (50.3 µs both). The table above was regenerated in one run (benchmarks/compare_marlin.py --autotune, interleaved rounds); benchmarks/l2_residency_check.py reproduces the flush and DRAM-byte checks.

Without group-wise parameters (per-tensor scale), TFLOP/s, 4096³: 36.9 (cuBLAS fp16 43.6); 1 × 4096 × 4096 decode: ~49 µs with the weights L2-resident (the kernel is latency-bound there, 3.7× faster than cuBLAS). Numbers from a laptop GPU are only meaningful relative to the same machine and clock state.
